\begin{PRSabstract}
Instrument size constrains visualization and instrument manoeuvrability during trans-oral robotic surgery (TORS) for cleft palate repair. This paper demonstrates feasibility of  TORS with miniature 3 mm wristed instruments and provides a comparison with standard 8 mm instruments using simulated repair in a live porcine model. Procedures were performed by one surgeon with analysis including three 3 mm and four 8 mm trials. Endoscopic video was analysis to quantify differences in visual field obstruction, procedure duration, and granular suture timings. The median of visual field obstruction was 10.4\% with 3 mm instruments and 23.9\% with 8 mm instruments. Across 62 stitches, the mean suture placement times were 7.1 versus 4.1 minutes, measured from needle pickup to tightening of the final throw. Median robotic procedure duration was 115.9 versus 75.5 minutes. \verifytext{\textbf{[Repair completion and closure quality: pending operative review.]}} The smaller instruments were allow for less visual field obstruction but present longer suturing times in this experimental setup. Specific quantitative interpretation is limited by the small population size of surgeon and models as well as differences in operative steps and tool usage but does not disprove feasibility. Miniaturization of tools while maintaining robotic dexterity opens the door for innovative intervention design especially in and through the infant oral cavity.

\PRSkeywords{transoral robotic surgery; simulated cleft palate repair; miniature instruments; porcine model}
\end{PRSabstract}

\section*{Miniature Instruments for Simulated Cleft Palate Repair}
Cleft palate repair requires delicate tissue handling within the limited space of the oral cavity. Simulator studies demonstrated the value of robotic articulation and visualization, while identifying instrument size as a constraint~\cite{podolsky2017simulator,podolsky2017robotic}.

Compact, cable-driven 3 mm instruments were subsequently evaluated using reachability analysis and a synthetic cleft palate model. They reduced collisions and visual field obstruction relative to 8 mm instruments~\cite{maguire2025}. The present study evaluated simulated repair in living porcine tissue.

\section*{Discussion}
The smaller instruments occupied less of the visual field. Suture placement and procedure durations were shorter with 8 mm instruments. Timing difference in the overall procedures can be attributed largely to the manufacturing and in-group consistency of the tools. Tool changes take significantly longer with the 3 mm instruments and are prone to failure. These times are represented in dissection and the gap between procedure stages.

While camera distance and specific operative steps varied across trials, effectiveness and benefits of smaller tools can still be evaluated qualitatively. The image-area comparison reflects the recorded operative view as deemed appropriate by the surgeon for the particular tools and stage of operation. Standardized camera and tool placement relative to the porcine model would not accurately represent the best operating conditions for a surgeon given variation in defect length, oral cavity size, and hand-eye coordination of the robot and the operator.

The standard dVRK provides no force feedback and tissue forces were not directly measured. The surgeon reported tearing of the mucosa during grasping at the defect edge in both groups. \verifytext{The 3 mm elevator resembled instruments used in conventional cleft repair.} Despite their larger shafts, the 8 mm micro-tipped forceps and Cichon graspers had smaller jaw end effectors, facilitating knot throws. Their smoother jaw surfaces also appeared to snag the suture less often.

This single-surgeon study was conducted with small and unequal population sizes. Learning effect, procedure order, repair complexity, and stitch counts may explain part of the timing difference between tool sizes.

Camera calibration was unavailable so physical metrics relating to the cavity and defect could not be determined reliably from video alone. Wrist friction varies with pitch and cable tension and as a result robot joint efforts cannot establish tissue force or injury~\cite{ho2024friction}. The use of surgically created palate defects in the porcine models limits extrapolation to infant anatomy, healing, and speech and aesthetic outcomes.

\verifytext{The findings identify a visualization advantage and instrument-handling tradeoffs relevant to robotic surgery in confined spaces. Further assessment of repair completion and closure quality is needed to establish the clinical relevance of these findings.}
