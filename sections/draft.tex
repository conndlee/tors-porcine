\WIPsection{Miniature Instruments for Simulated Cleft Palate Repair}
\WIPsubsection{Background}
Cleft palate repair requires tissue dissection and suturing in a restricted oral workspace. A high-fidelity simulator provided an anatomically realistic platform for rehearsal.\supercite{podolsky2017simulator} Earlier robotic simulator work demonstrated the value of wrist articulation and visualization, while identifying instrument size as a constraint.\supercite{podolsky2017robotic} Robotic visualization and wrist articulation may assist these tasks, although large instruments can obstruct the view and contact adjacent structures. Maguire and colleagues developed compact, cable-driven, pin-jointed 3 mm instruments for the da Vinci Research Kit and evaluated them using a high-fidelity synthetic cleft palate simulator. Their study combined geometric reachability analysis with physical experiments and reported fewer collisions and reduced image obstruction compared with 8 mm instruments.\supercite{maguire2025}

\WIPsection{Discussion}
\WIPsubsection{Relevance to Transoral Reconstruction}
The clinical motivation is to make dexterous transoral reconstruction practical in a small workspace while retaining sufficient capability for dissection and closure. The earlier simulator investigation establishes the engineering rationale; this porcine study addressed a subsequent preclinical question about operative performance in living tissue.\supercite{maguire2025} \textbf{[Interpret the observed completion and closure findings alongside the measured differences in visualization, speed, and tissue interaction.]}

A smaller instrument profile is useful only when it preserves effective tissue manipulation. The porcine repairs extended evaluation from a reproducible synthetic workspace to living tissue, where exposure, tissue handling, and closure were assessed together. \textbf{[One-sentence interpretation of the principal observed advantage or tradeoff.]}

\WIPsubsection{Measurement and Study Limitations}
Image-area obstruction offers a practical view of instrument interference with the operative field. Its interpretation requires comparable camera position, magnification, task, and image region. Camera calibration was unavailable, so physical bite depth and spacing require an independent scale or measurement method. Absolute three-dimensional tissue reconstruction cannot be assumed from stereo recordings alone.

Friction in the 3 mm wrist varies with pitch and cable tension.\supercite{ho2024friction} Logged joint effort is therefore not a direct measurement of tissue force. Any force estimate requires verification of topic contents, units, instrument-specific transmission, friction, and calibration. Jaw effort describes a different interaction from needle-driving or traction force. Effort without force calibration represents a proxy for tissue interaction; video deformation alone cannot establish lower force or reduced tissue injury.

This is a small, single-surgeon series. The single operator limited assessment of variation between surgeons. Repeated sutures were clustered within repairs; \textbf{[unit of analysis, aggregation or clustering method, and uncertainty estimation]}. Trial order, learning, anatomy, and setup may influence comparisons. Similar closure scores alone would not establish equivalence. Porcine simulated defects also limit inference about infant anatomy, healing, speech, and clinical safety.
