\begin{PRSabstract}
Miniature wristed instruments were evaluated for simulated cleft palate repair in a live porcine model. The available main comparison included three recordings using 3 mm instruments and four using 8 mm instruments. The median of the recording-level median image occlusions was 10.39\% versus 23.93\%. Across 61 annotated stitches, the mean of the per-pig mean stitch durations was 7.1 versus 4.2 minutes. Smaller instruments therefore provided a less obstructed recorded view, while suturing took longer in this series. \textbf{[Add repair completion, closure quality, and a supported conclusion.]}
\PRSkeywords{transoral robotic surgery; simulated cleft palate repair; miniature instruments; porcine model}
\end{PRSabstract}

\WIPsection{Miniature Instruments for Simulated Cleft Palate Repair}
\WIPsubsection{Background}
Cleft palate repair requires delicate tissue handling within a confined oral workspace. Simulator studies demonstrated the value of robotic articulation and visualization, while identifying instrument size as a constraint.\supercite{podolsky2017simulator,podolsky2017robotic}

Compact, cable-driven 3 mm instruments were subsequently evaluated using reachability analysis and a synthetic cleft palate model. They reduced collisions and image obstruction relative to 8 mm instruments.\supercite{maguire2025} The present study evaluated simulated repair in living porcine tissue.

\WIPsection{Discussion}
\WIPsubsection{Interpretation}
The smaller instruments occupied less of the recorded image, but the timing results favored 8 mm instruments. Instrument miniaturization therefore requires assessment alongside task completion, closure quality, and operative time.

Camera distance and task mix differed between recordings. The image-area comparison reflects the recorded operative view; it does not establish an isolated effect of diameter. The pooled-frame histogram also weights longer recordings more heavily than shorter recordings.

\WIPsubsection{Limitations}
This was a small, single-surgeon series with unequal group sizes. Learning, procedure order, repair complexity, and stitch counts may explain part of the timing difference. The recordings and stitches were not independent observations at the same level.

Camera calibration was unavailable. Physical closure measurements required an independent scale. Wrist friction varies with pitch and cable tension, so logged effort alone cannot establish tissue force or injury.\supercite{ho2024friction} Porcine simulated defects also limit inference about infant anatomy, healing, and speech.

\WIPsubsection{Conclusion}
The current results describe a less obstructed view with 3 mm instruments and longer annotated stitch durations. \textbf{[Integrate verified completion and closure findings before drawing a clinical conclusion.]}
